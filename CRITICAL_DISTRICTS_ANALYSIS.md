## Economic Impact (Estimated)

### Direct Costs of Groundwater Depletion
- **Deeper Borewells:** ₹1.5-3 lakhs per borewell (every 5-10 years)
- **Higher Pumping Costs:** 30-50% increase in electricity bills
- **Failed Borewells:** 20-30% of new borewells unsuccessful

### Indirect Costs
- **Agricultural Loss:** Reduced crop yields, shift to less water-intensive crops
- **Migration:** Rural-urban migration due to water scarcity
- **Health:** Increased water contamination in shallow aquifers
- **Industrial:** Production constraints, relocation costs

## Policy Recommendations

### Immediate (0-6 months)
1. **Emergency Declaration:** Declare Sagar, Indore, and Bhopal districts as groundwater emergency zones
2. **Ban New Extraction:** Moratorium on new borewell permits in Critical districts
3. **Mandatory Monitoring:** Install real-time water level sensors in critical wells
4. **Water Budgeting:** Calculate district-wise sustainable extraction limits

### Short Term (6-24 months)
1. **Artificial Recharge:** Accelerate construction of check dams, percolation tanks, recharge wells
2. **Pricing Reform:** Implement volumetric pricing for groundwater (metered borewells)
3. **Crop Planning:** Incentivize less water-intensive crops in Critical districts
4. **Awareness Campaign:** Mass media campaign on water conservation

### Medium Term (2-5 years)
1. **Surface Water Infrastructure:** Expand canal networks to reduce groundwater dependency
2. **Wastewater Reuse:** Treat and reuse urban wastewater for non-potable uses
3. **Aquifer Mapping:** High-resolution 3D aquifer characterization
4. **Legal Framework:** Strengthen groundwater regulation and enforcement

### Long Term (5-10 years)
1. **Inter-Basin Transfer:** Explore options for water import from surplus basins
2. **Demand Management:** Shift to water-efficient industries and agriculture
3. **Climate Adaptation:** Build resilience to changing rainfall patterns
4. **Institutional Capacity:** Strengthen groundwater governance institutions

## Conclusion

**Nearly half of Madhya Pradesh's monitored groundwater wells are in Critical declining status**, with some districts showing >95% Critical wells. This represents a **severe and urgent water security crisis** requiring immediate multi-level intervention. The situation in Sagar, Indore, and Bhopal districts is particularly alarming and warrants emergency status.

However, the success of Guna district (4.5% Critical) demonstrates that sustainable groundwater management is achievable. Studying and replicating Guna's success factors could provide a roadmap for other districts.

**Time is critical.** Without immediate action, many of these wells will go dry within 2-5 years, causing severe agricultural, economic, and social disruption.
